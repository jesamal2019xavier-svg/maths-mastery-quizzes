Venn Diagrams

Section 1

Guided Practice

Fill in the Venn diagram regions using the information given. Remember: the intersection (middle) contains people/items that belong to both groups — don't count them twice! Work through Question 1 with your teacher, then try the rest with a partner.

1
In a class of 30 students:
• 18 study PE  • 12 study Art  • 5 study both

Fill in the four regions of the Venn diagram.
✓ Mark Scheme PE only = 18 − 5 = 13
Both = 5 (given)
Art only = 12 − 5 = 7
Neither = 30 − 13 − 5 − 7 = 5

Check: 13 + 5 + 7 + 5 = 30 ✓
2
A group of 40 people were surveyed about pets:
• 22 own a Dog  • 15 own a Cat  • 6 own both  • Some own neither

Fill in the Venn diagram, then state how many own neither.
✓ Mark Scheme Dog only = 22 − 6 = 16
Both = 6 (given)
Cat only = 15 − 6 = 9
Neither = 40 − 16 − 6 − 9 = 9

Check: 16 + 6 + 9 + 9 = 40 ✓
3
50 students were asked if they like Maths and/or Science.
• 9 like neither  • 14 like both  • 30 like Maths

How many like Science? Fill in all regions.
✓ Mark Scheme Neither = 9 (given)
Both = 14 (given)
Maths only = 30 − 14 = 16
Total accounted for = 50 − 9 = 41 in circles
Science only = 41 − 30 = 11, so Science total = 11 + 14 = 25

Check: 16 + 14 + 11 + 9 = 50 ✓
Section 2

Probability from Venn Diagrams

Once a Venn diagram is filled in, we can calculate probabilities. The denominator is always the total of all values in the diagram (the universal set). Use the key notation below, then answer each question.

NotationMeaningWhich region(s)?
P(A)Probability of ALeft circle (both parts)
P(A∩B)P(A and B) — intersectionMiddle only
P(A∪B)P(A or B) — unionEverything in either circle
P(A')P(not A) — complementEverything outside A's circle
Use this diagram for Questions 1–4:
80 people were asked if they have a driving licence (D) and/or a passport (P).
1
Easy
A person is chosen at random. Find P(D) — the probability they have a driving licence.
✓ Answer D includes "D only" and "Both": 35 + 21 = 56
P(D) = 56/80 = 7/10
2
Easy
Find P(D∩P) — the probability a person has both a driving licence and a passport.
✓ Answer The intersection (middle) = 21
P(D∩P) = 21/80
3
Medium
Find P(D∪P) — the probability a person has a driving licence or a passport (or both).
✓ Answer Union = everyone inside either circle = 35 + 21 + 14 = 70
P(D∪P) = 70/80 = 7/8

Alternative: P(D∪P) = 1 − P(neither) = 1 − 10/80 = 70/80 ✓
4
Medium
Find P(D') — the probability a person does not have a driving licence.
✓ Answer D' = everything outside D's circle = P only + Neither = 14 + 10 = 24
P(D') = 24/80 = 3/10

Or: P(D') = 1 − P(D) = 1 − 56/80 = 24/80 ✓
5
Hard
A different survey of 60 students:
• P(plays guitar) = 1/3   • P(plays piano) = 1/4   • P(plays both) = 1/12

Find the number of students who play neither instrument. Draw and complete the Venn diagram.
✓ Answer Guitar total = 1/3 × 60 = 20  |  Piano total = 1/4 × 60 = 15  |  Both = 1/12 × 60 = 5
Guitar only = 20 − 5 = 15
Piano only = 15 − 5 = 10
Neither = 60 − 15 − 5 − 10 = 30
Section 3

Spot the Mistake

Each of the following contains a deliberate error. Can you find it and explain what the student should have done instead? Try before revealing the answer!

⚠ Mistake 1
Question: 50 people, 28 like Tea, 20 like Coffee, 8 like both. A student writes:

"Tea only = 28, Coffee only = 20, Both = 8, Neither = 50 − 28 − 20 − 8 = −6"
✓ The Error The student used the total for Tea (28) and Coffee (20) without subtracting the intersection first. They counted the 8 "both" people three times.

Correct working:
Tea only = 28 − 8 = 20  |  Coffee only = 20 − 8 = 12  |  Both = 8
Neither = 50 − 20 − 12 − 8 = 10

Key rule: Always subtract the intersection from each circle total before placing values.
⚠ Mistake 2
Question: The diagram shows: A only = 12, Both = 7, B only = 9, Neither = 4. Total = 32.
A student writes: "P(A) = 12/32"
✓ The Error P(A) means the probability of being in set A — which includes both the "A only" region and the intersection.

Correct: P(A) = (12 + 7)/32 = 19/32

Remember: A circle contains two regions — "only" and "both".
⚠ Mistake 3
Question: 40 people. A only = 10, B only = 15, Both = 8.
A student says: "Neither = 40 − 10 − 15 = 15"
✓ The Error The student forgot to subtract the intersection (Both = 8) from the total.

Correct: Neither = 40 − 10 − 15 − 8 = 7

Always subtract all four regions. The intersection is its own region!
⚠ Mistake 4
Question: P(A∩B) = 3/20. A student says: "This means 3 people like A and 20 people like B."
✓ The Error P(A∩B) = 3/20 is a probability fraction, not a count. The numerator (3) and denominator (20) do not individually represent group sizes.

Correct interpretation: If 3/20 of the total are in the intersection, and total = e.g. 40, then 6 people like both.

Never read a probability fraction as individual group sizes.

3
Explain in your own words: Why do we subtract the intersection when filling in a Venn diagram? Give an example to support your answer.
✓ Model Answer When we're told "18 people like PE," those 18 include people who like PE only AND people who like both. If we put 18 into the PE circle without adjusting, we'd count the "both" people twice — once in each circle. So we subtract the intersection first to find how many like PE alone.

Example: 18 like PE, 5 like both → PE only = 18−5 = 13.
